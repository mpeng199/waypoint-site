Not money, not an endorsement, and not your staff time beyond one conversation. Here is exactly what we want, what you get back, and who is accountable for what.

The gap we are trying to close

New York already built the infrastructure. Hospitals are required to offer financial assistance to patients under certain incomes. The state runs an independent appeal for denied claims. Free counselors handle coverage disputes in dozens of languages, and national nonprofits will file a charity care application on someone's behalf at no cost.

Almost nobody who qualifies uses any of it. Not because they were turned away, but because nobody told them it was there. The help is real, it is free, and it is invisible. Waypoint exists to close that one gap.

Three things, and any one of them is worth a conversation

Your materials. Flyers, referral cards, or a QR code we can put in front of people at libraries, senior centers, faith spaces and community events. We cover the printing and we do the distribution.

A name we can route to. One person or one line we send people toward, so a referral is a handoff instead of a hope.

Twenty minutes of review, if you can spare it. A read of our volunteer routing sheet so we know we are not overstating anything. A read and a reply, not a supervision commitment. We would far rather be corrected by you now than be wrong in front of somebody who needed us to be right.

What you get back

Reach you do not have to staff. Our students speak the languages their blocks speak. A conversation about a frightening bill rarely survives a stranger translating it, and almost never survives a leaflet.

People who would not otherwise arrive. Nobody is asked for an immigration status. A hospital cannot weigh it when it looks at financial assistance, and anything touching immigration law goes to a professional rather than a student's best guess.

Counts you can put in front of a board. Every quarter: how many people we sent you and what they asked about. Categories, never names.

What a referral actually looks like

A volunteer listens. Someone describes a problem in their own words. A bill they cannot pay. A denial letter they cannot parse. A prescription they have quietly stopped filling.

They name the door, not the answer. The volunteer matches the situation to the right organization and explains what that organization does. They never assess eligibility and never interpret a document.

They make the connection happen. Dialing the helpline together, starting the form on the spot, walking someone to a counselor who is there at the event. A flyer handed over is not a handoff.

Who is accountable

  • A named adult anchors the program. A faculty advisor is the contact when something needs one.
  • The school's policy covers off-site volunteer activity, confirmed in writing.
  • Volunteers work in pairs or groups, never alone. Community-facing events are co-staffed by a certified professional who handles anything regulated.
  • We keep anonymous counts and nothing else. No names, no medical details, and no documents: volunteers take custody of no paperwork at all.

The boundary our volunteers work inside is published in full on the student page, so you can read the rules before you decide whether to trust them with your name.

Where we actually are

We have not held our first event. No volunteer will speak to anyone about a bill until our routing materials have been read by someone with real health-insurance-law knowledge, and distribution of reviewed printed material is happening first, deliberately.

That is the honest position, and it is also the offer. Whoever partners with us early shapes how the whole thing runs, and is named in the first semester report.

What we tell everyone we meet

We are trained student volunteers. We help you find the free programs and professionals in New York that handle medical bills and insurance denials. We are not doctors, lawyers, benefits counselors, or insurance experts. We do not read your bills, fill out your forms, or tell you what you qualify for. We connect you to people who do that, and they do it for free. We never charge for anything.

Talk to us

Materials we can distribute, a person we can route people to, a space, or twenty minutes to read our routing sheet. Tell us which and we will come back with something specific. You can also write to waypointoutreach@gmail.com.
