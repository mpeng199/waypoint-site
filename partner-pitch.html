WAYPOINT

Medical Bills & Coverage Navigation · New York City

We help New Yorkers buried in medical bills or fighting a denied insurance claim reach the free experts who can actually help. A trained student volunteer corps, working by warm handoff, across New York City.

The gap we close

New York already has the infrastructure. Hospitals are required to offer financial assistance to patients under certain incomes. The state runs an independent appeal process for denied claims. Free counselors handle coverage disputes in dozens of languages, and national nonprofits will file a charity care application on a patient's behalf at no cost.

Almost none of the people who qualify ever use any of it. Not because they were turned away, but because nobody told them it existed. The help is real, it is free, and it is invisible. Waypoint is built to close that one gap: we find people who are stuck, and we walk them to the professionals who already handle this.

We are trained student volunteers. We help you find the free programs and professionals in New York that handle medical bills and insurance denials. We are not doctors, lawyers, benefits counselors, or insurance experts. We do not read your bills, fill out your forms, or tell you what you qualify for. We connect you to people who do that, and they do it for free. We never charge for anything.

What we are asking you for

Not money. Not an endorsement. Not your staff time beyond one conversation. In return you get distribution you do not have to staff, and a quarterly count of how many people we sent your way and what they were asking about.

Three things, and any one of them is worth a conversation

  • Your materials. Flyers, referral cards, or a QR code we can put in front of people at libraries, senior centers, faith spaces, and community events. We cover printing and distribution.
  • A name we can route to. One person or line we send people toward, so a referral is a handoff instead of a hope.
  • Twenty minutes of review, if you can spare it. A read of our volunteer routing sheet, so we know we are not overstating anything. A read and a reply, not a supervision commitment.

What a referral actually looks like

  1. A volunteer listens Someone describes a problem in their own words. A bill they cannot pay. A denial letter they do not understand. A prescription they have stopped filling.
  2. They identify the door, not the answer Volunteers match the situation to the right organisation and explain what that organisation does. They never assess eligibility or interpret documents.
  3. They make the connection happen Dialling the helpline together. Starting the form on the spot. Introducing someone to a counselor present at the event. A flyer handed over is not a handoff.

Why now

We have not held our first event yet, and no volunteer will speak to anyone about a bill until our routing materials have been reviewed by someone with real health-insurance-law knowledge. Distribution of reviewed printed materials is happening first, deliberately. Whoever partners with us early shapes how the whole thing runs and is named in the first semester report.

Who is accountable

  • Faculty advisor. A named adult contact at the school anchors the programme.
  • Coverage. Off-site volunteer activity is covered under the school's policy, confirmed in writing.
  • Supervision. Volunteers work in pairs or groups. Community-facing events are co-staffed by a certified professional who handles anything regulated.
  • Data. We record anonymous aggregate counts only. No names, no medical details, no documents held.
