The help is already here. No one walks you to it.

New York is full of free care, free counsel, free food, free rides. Most of the people who need it never reach the door.

All around them. Out of reach.

Senior centers, benefits counselors, clinics, Medicare help, food programs, transit fares. All free, all open, all quietly going unused. Not because the city built too little. Because nobody walks people to the door.

So we became the way through.

Waypoint is a trained student volunteer corps. We bring older, low-income, and immigrant New Yorkers to the free health resources and the professionals who already serve them. We are not building a second system. We are the introduction the first one never makes.

A resource navigation event

What it looks like on a Saturday morning.

It starts with somebody walking in out of the heat. A branch library on a Saturday, a table near the door, a few students, and no clipboard in anyone's face. Most people who come in are not there for us. They came for the air conditioning, the computers, a book for a grandchild. We say hello anyway.

The conversation is the whole job. A student asks what has been hard lately, in whatever language that conversation works best in. Someone mentions a prescription they stopped filling, or a letter they could not read, or a parent who is alone from Monday to Friday.

Then we say what exists, plainly. Not what you qualify for, which is not ours to decide. What exists: this program, that office, this number, and roughly who it is meant for. Enough for someone to recognise themselves in it and say yes, that sounds like me.

And then the short walk across the room. The certified counselor is already sitting at the next table, because we asked them to be there before we asked you for a room. The student makes the introduction and steps back. The regulated work starts exactly where our part stops.

Nobody leaves holding a pamphlet. They leave with one thing they can act on. An appointment, a name, an address, a number that a person actually answers, and someone who will check that it worked.

We share the way. We never play the doctor.

We are trained student volunteers who connect you to free and low-cost health resources that already exist in NYC. We are not doctors, nurses, social workers, or benefits counselors. We share information; we do not give medical or legal advice. We never charge for anything.

This is printed on every table sign, every flyer, and said out loud at every event. It is the first thing we teach and the last thing we would ever bend.

Who we send

Trained to know exactly where the line is.

Every student is vetted before they meet anyone. Onboarding, parental consent, a background check where the setting calls for one, and whatever additional training you require of your own volunteers. Your rules layered on top of ours, never instead of them.

They commit by the semester, not by the afternoon. Students join a cohort with a minimum commitment and a real schedule, and we staff in stable pairs so that no single volunteer is irreplaceable and no shift depends on one teenager's week going well.

When the semester ends, we tell you. Summer is an honest pause with a handover, not a slow disappearance. You will have our calendar before you plan anything around us.

What they will not do is the part that protects you. They do not enroll anyone, fill in an application, or touch a protected system. They do not give medical or legal advice, and they do not discuss anyone's diagnosis or medication. They are never alone with a vulnerable adult; they work in pairs or groups with an adult present. They write down no names and no health details. If a question touches immigration status, they stop and route it to a professional.

That last part is not caution, it is the law. Enrollment assistance in New York is done by certified assistors who train, sit an exam, and work for an authorized agency. A student volunteer cannot legally do it, so we built the program so that they never need to.

Reach

The people you have been trying to reach all year.

Our students speak the languages your block speaks. Multilingual volunteers are the point of this, not a bonus feature. A conversation about a benefit someone has never heard of rarely survives being translated by a stranger, and almost never survives a leaflet.

Nobody is asked for a status. We route to services that serve people regardless of immigration status, and anything that touches immigration goes to an immigration professional rather than a student's best guess. Wrong information there costs a family far more than the appointment was ever worth.

Behind every referral is a directory we maintain. A curated set of free and low-cost services across the five boroughs, each entry carrying the date it was last checked and a named human on the other end wherever one exists. It is re-verified every semester by someone whose job that is. A directory that sends an eighty-year-old to a disconnected number is worse than no directory at all.

And you get numbers you can actually use. After every event we hand you counts: how many people came, what they asked about, how many were walked to a professional. Categories, never names, no health information, nothing that could identify anyone. Aggregate by design, and exactly the shape of thing your board or your funder asks you for in the spring.

For partners

One room, one morning, one counselor.

A room you already have. A library branch, a senior center, a community or faith hall, on a day you are open anyway. We bring the students, the materials, the table signs, and the directory. You do not staff it and you do not fund it.

A certified counselor in the room with us. Yours, or one we bring. This is the piece that makes the morning worth someone's time, and it is the piece that keeps the regulated work in regulated hands.

We work inside your supervision and your coverage. Students follow your rules, with an adult present, doing only what this page says they do. Nothing about the arrangement asks you to extend cover to something new or to take on a risk you were not already carrying.

And we have not done this yet. That is the honest part. Waypoint has not held its first event. We are looking for the branch that hosts it, and whoever says yes will shape how the whole thing runs, be named in the first semester report, and be the reason there is a second one.
